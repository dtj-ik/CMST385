The Science behind Therapy Cats

More Than Just Comfort

Therapy cats do more than provide comfort; their presence can create measurable changes in the body and mind. Research suggest that calm interactions with cats may help reduce stress, support emotional balance, and encourage connection, making feline-assisted therapy a meaningful part of wellness-focused environments.

Featured Study

Photo Credit: Pixabay

Can Cat Companionship Reduce Loneliness?

By Sanderson et al., 2024 - The Journals of Gerontology: Series B

This study explored whether fostering a shelter cat could improve loneliness and well-being in older adults living alone. Results showed that loneliness significantly improved after four months, suggesting that cat companionship may offer meaningful emotional support for older adults.

Cat Assisted Therapy Overview

Research focused on how interactions with cats affect the body.

Feline-assisted therapy: Integrating contact with cats into treatment plans

By Tomaszewska, Bomert, & Wilkiewicz-Wawro, 2017

This review article explains feline-assisted therapy as a contact-based therapy involving cats and discusses how it may support people with physical and psychological conditions. It also notes that therapy cats should be healthy, vaccinated, sterilized, and have a well-developed temperament.

Putting Cats on the Spectrum: A Scoping Review of the Role of Cats in Therapy and Companionship for Autistic People

By Cleary et al., 2023

This scoping review brings together studies about cats in animal-assisted interventions, assistance roles, and companionship, especially for autistic individuals.

Physiological Effects

Examining the physiological imapct of therapy cats.

Effects of Interactions with Cats in Domestic Environment on the Psychological and Physiological State of Their Owners

By Nagasawa et al., 2023

This study measured heart rate, cortisol, oxytocin, heart rate variability, and emotional state during human-cat interaction. The results showed that free interaction with cats decreased emotional arousal and affected physiological responses in owners.

Does cat attachment have an effect on human health? A comparison between owners and volunteers

By Dinis & Martins, 2016

This article discusses heart rate, blood pressure, and human health in relation to cat interaction and attachment.
